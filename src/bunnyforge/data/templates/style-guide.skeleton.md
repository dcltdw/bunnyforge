## Register and tone

<!--
- What is the emotional register of this setting in one sentence?
- Name three things that are permitted here that are not permitted in generic
  fantasy, and three that are forbidden.
- Is this world hopeful, bleak, or something with a name of its own? What does
  that choice rule *out*?
- Which published works or authors sit closest to the voice you want? Name two
  or three, and for each say what you are taking — register, pacing, how
  violence lands, how much is left unsaid — rather than naming the work whole.
- Name one that is *close but wrong*. The near miss calibrates faster than the
  matches do: it marks where the boundary runs.
-->


## Prose rules

<!--
- Person and tense for narration, and for read-aloud text.
- Words and constructions you never want to see. Be specific enough to check.
- How long is too long for a paragraph a GM reads aloud at the table?
-->


## Description

<!--
- What does a place description lead with — sight, sound, smell, or something else?
- How much detail before it becomes furniture the players will not remember?
-->


## Describing peoples and cultures

<!--
- What structural slot do cultures occupy in your setting? (Species? Nations?
  Something else? Nothing?)
- What are you deliberately *not* doing here? Write this down before you need it.
-->


## Dialogue

<!--
- How do registers differ between speakers — formality, class, region, species?
- Does your setting have in-world vocabulary? If so, list it with glosses, and
  say when a speaker would use it rather than the common word.
-->


## System

<!--
- Which ruleset, which edition, and which house rules touch prose?
- How are mechanical terms written in narrative text?
-->


## Naming conventions

<!--
- What naming tradition does each people or region draw on?
- What is the difference between a place name and a personal name here?
- Which names are already taken and must not be reused?
-->


## Anachronism and drift

<!--
- Which real-world words break the spell for you? List the ones you keep catching.
- When a stock genre element has no place in your setting, what happens — is it
  removed, renamed, or replaced?
-->
