## The core principle

- Write your one-paragraph statement of what you prepare *instead of* a plot.
- What is the test that tells you a piece of prep is a situation rather than a
  script?

## Ways in, ways out

- How many entry points does a situation need before you consider it ready?
- How many exits, and what makes an exit unsatisfying?

## The consequence cap

- How far may a single session's events propagate through the setting?
- What is never allowed to change without the table's involvement?

## The briefing template

Copy this per situation. Sections you leave empty are questions, not answers —
flag them rather than inventing content to fill them.

# <Situation Name>

## In one line
## The parties
## The collision
## What is already in motion
## Three ways in
## Three ways out
## If the party does not engage
## What the party will probably do that is not on this list

## Voice notes

## From briefing to table

- What do you actually carry to the table — the whole briefing, or a cut-down of it?
- What do you write down *during* play, and where does it go afterwards?

## Do not

- List the failure modes you personally keep repeating. This section is worth
  more than the rest of the file combined, and only you can write it.
